How the work moves

Every program follows the same shape, whether it's a 15-day sprint or a 45-day track.

  • 01

    Explore a real problem

    You start on an actual brief, not a toy exercise — the same kind of problem a business would bring to us.

  • 02

    Build a working solution

    Guided, hands-on building with regular check-ins — the goal is something that runs, not just something that's graded.

  • 03

    Grow into the next role

    You leave with a shipped project, a certificate, and — for strong performers — a reference for what comes next.

About Orythavion

Helping people build skills, and businesses build solutions.

Two goals, one company — internships and training on one side, technology projects on the other, feeding into each other.

Who we are

Orythavion Technologies is a technology company built around two connected goals: giving people practical, hands-on skills, and giving businesses working solutions. Internships and training are how we do the first; projects are how we do the second — and often the same work serves both.

Mission

To close the gap between what's taught and what's actually used — by putting people on real projects early, and by building technology that solves real problems for real businesses.

What we currently do

  • InternshipsShort, structured, project-based tracks across frontend, backend, data, and machine learning.
  • TrainingFocused courses in Python, data analytics, AI/ML, and web development for beginners and early-career learners.
  • In-house projectsTools and products we build ourselves — some of which start as intern work.
  • Early client workSmall technology projects for outside businesses, taken on as they come in.

Real work, not simulations

Every internship brief is built the way an actual client brief would be — scoped, imperfect, and open-ended.

Small enough to notice you

We're early-stage on purpose right now, so feedback is direct and mentors know your name.

Built to be shown, not just graded

The output of every program is something you can put in a portfolio or a resume — not just a score.
